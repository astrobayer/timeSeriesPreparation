timeSeriesPreparation.checkAlignment

Report a time lag between observation and reference; never shift.

Syntax

report = timeSeriesPreparation.checkAlignment(obs, ref)
report = timeSeriesPreparation.checkAlignment(obs, ref, MaxLag = 60, Threshold = 1)

Description

A diagnostic for timing between two series (CONCEPT 6.1, row 3; v3 F2). v2 searched the lag over a full revolution and silently shifted the model by it. Near-periodic 1/rev signals can lock that search onto the wrong lobe, and a real physical lag (the thermosphere responding late to its driver) was absorbed instead of reported. Here the search is bounded to +/- MaxLag (60 s, far below one revolution), and the result is a report only. No series is shifted; correcting a timing error is the caller's explicit decision, for example by fixing the time system.

Both series are resampled onto one uniform grid of spacing Step, anchored at the first observation epoch over the common span. The rule is the one align uses: no interpolation across a hole. Each column is AC-coupled (its mean over the common valid rows removed). For every lag L = -K..K (K = MaxLag/Step) the normalised cross-correlation is taken over the valid pairs:

r(L) = sum a_i b_(i-L) / sqrt(sum a_i^2 * sum b_(i-L)^2)

A parabola through the peak and its two neighbours refines the lag below the grid step. A positive lag means that the observation lags the reference: obs(t) matches ref(t - lag).

Verdict per column: "aligned" |lag| <= Threshold (1 s). "offset" Threshold < |lag| <= MaxLag/2; warns. "gross" |lag| > MaxLag/2, or the peak lies on the edge of the search; warns. Fix the time systems. "undetermined" no positive peak, or fewer valid pairs than lags searched (e.g. no common span); warns.

Accuracy

On one day at 1 s with a 1/rev signal plus 40 broadband tones, a NumPy transcription of this estimator recovers an injected lag of 7.0 s to 4e-6 s and one of 7.3 s to 1e-3 s. The parabola's bias grows towards half a step (measured 2026-09-23). TestCheckAlignment holds the 7.0 s case to 0.05 s and the 7.3 s case to 0.01 s. Each correlation is a sum over one lag's valid pairs, and each column is summed on its own, so a batched call equals the per-column calls bit for bit.

Example

Two identical 1 Hz series: lag 0, verdict "aligned", no warning.

aux = @(p) struct("obsNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableFieldNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableUnits", struct("lin_accl_x", "m/s2"), "fileName", p + "_2020-01-01_C_04.txt", "product", p);
t = int64(0:3599)' * int64(1e9);
x = sin(double(t) * 1e-9 / 30) + cos(double(t) * 1e-9 / 7);
report = timeSeriesPreparation.checkAlignment(timeSeriesPreparation.Arc(t, x, aux("ACT1B")), timeSeriesPreparation.Arc(t, x, aux("MOD1B")));

Limitations

A lag beyond MaxLag cannot be measured. With a smooth signal the peak then sits on the search edge ("gross"). With broadband content a side lobe inside the window can be reported instead: a 90 s lag of the 1/rev plus 40-tone test signal shows as 7.27 s with peak 0.50, where a true lag peaks at 0.9999 (NumPy, 2026-09-23). A low peak is the sign. One constant lag per column over the whole arc; a drifting clock shows as a broadened peak, not as a drift. Columns pair by position. Units are not compared, because the correlation is scale-free; align compares them.
