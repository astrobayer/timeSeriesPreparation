timeSeriesPreparation.observability

Identifiability metrics of a design before the fit, and the minimum-data gate.

Syntax

report = timeSeriesPreparation.observability(arc, X)
report = timeSeriesPreparation.observability(arc, X, Names = names, MinWeightFraction = 0.5)

Description

The gate stage (CONCEPT 6.1, row 6). An application calls it with the regressors it is about to fit, and refuses or downgrades parameters the data cannot separate. v2 fitted bias and scale on quiet arcs, where the two estimates correlate at -1 (CONCEPT 8). No fit happens here (CONCEPT 3.2); the metrics describe the design.

Per axis m (column of arc.obs) the fit weight of a row is w = arc.weights, times 0 where obs(:, m) or any regressor is not finite, and 0 where the row was filled (CONCEPT 5), and the kept weight fraction is sum(w)/N.

Minimum-data gate (CONCEPT 5.2): if any axis keeps less than MinWeightFraction, the call refuses with an identifier naming the axes; it never returns NaN metrics for them. MinWeightFraction [] takes Config(arc).unc.minWeightFraction (0.5 for ACC1B, ACT1B, ACH1B, KBR1B, LRI1B; owner decision 2026-09-23). A product without a preset needs it given.

Metrics per axis, from the rows with w > 0, each scaled by sqrt(w): cond the condition number of X after scaling each column to unit length (Belsley, Kuh & Welsch 1980): above about 30 the columns are nearly dependent. paramCorr the correlation matrix of the least-squares estimates, from (X' W X)^-1, for X exactly as given. For [1, a] it is -mean(a)/sqrt(mean(a.^2)): -1 when a barely varies about a large mean. vif the variance inflation factor of each regressor, 1/(1 - R_j^2) with R_j^2 from regressing column j on the others and a constant (the weighted, centred form). NaN for a constant column. Above about 10 a parameter is poorly determined (Kutner et al. 2005, ch. 10). Whether to demean a regressor is the application's decision; the paramCorr of the demeaned design shows the effect.

Accuracy

Linear algebra in double: cond from the singular values, paramCorr and vif from inv of P x P matrices whose condition number is cond^2 at most. An exactly dependent design gives cond Inf (or near 1/eps), NaN paramCorr (rcond below eps) and Inf vif; an all-zero column leaves cond and paramCorr NaN. TestObservability pins the closed forms for [1, a] and for two regressors of known correlation to 1e-12.

Example

Bias and scale on a quiet arc: the estimates correlate near -1.

aux = struct("obsNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableFieldNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableUnits", struct("lin_accl_x", "m/s2"), "fileName", "ACT1B_2020-01-01_C_04.txt", "product", "ACT1B");
t = int64(0:999)' * int64(1e9);
model = 1e-7 + 1e-9 * sin((0:999)' / 100);
report = timeSeriesPreparation.observability(timeSeriesPreparation.Arc(t, model, aux), [ones(1000, 1), model], Names = ["bias" "scale"]);

Limitations

One design for all axes; per-axis designs need one call each. The thresholds quoted for cond and vif are rules of thumb and are not applied here: the application chooses.
