timeSeriesPreparation.qc.detectJumps

Change points in level or slope of whitened residuals by PELT with a BIC penalty; priors are assigned.

Syntax

[events, info] = timeSeriesPreparation.qc.detectJumps(r, Epoch = t)
[events, info] = timeSeriesPreparation.qc.detectJumps(r, Epoch = t, Model = "slope", Priors = ecl, Penalty = beta, MinSegment = 30)

Description

Minimises, per channel, the sum over segments of the least-squares cost plus a penalty beta per change point, by Pruned Exact Linear Time (Killick, Fearnhead and Eckley 2012), which equals the exhaustive optimum. The segment cost is the residual sum of squares about a constant ("level") or a straight line ("slope"). The default penalty is the BIC value (p + 1) sigma^2 log K, p = 1 (level) or 2 (slope) parameters per segment plus one for the change point's location, sigma the robust scale of the first differences (1.4826 MAD / sqrt(2)); Penalty replaces it (squared residual units). Each change point is a "jump" row: tStart = tEnd = first sample of the new segment, amplitude = new minus old level at the boundary (fitted lines for "slope"), sigma = sigma sqrt(1/n1 + 1/n2). A jump within PriorWindow_s of a prior row (eclipse, MAS1B tank event) takes its source and confidence 1; the others are "unassigned" with confidence erf(|amplitude|/sigma/sqrt(2)). Jumps are never cut from the data. NaN samples are removed before the segmentation and the indices mapped back. Ported from NonGravForces.qc 0.12.0 (X-05); numbers unchanged (X-05b, X-16).

Accuracy

Exact optimisation for the stated cost and penalty; the ported tests hold it to the exhaustive optimum of an independent Python oracle, bitwise in the indices. A step h in white noise sigma is found when h^2 n1 n2/(n1 + n2) > beta. Coloured noise without whitening gives spurious level changes: whiten first.

Example

A 4-sigma step in 1000 white samples.

x = randn(RandStream("twister", "Seed", 5), 1000, 1); x(601:end) = x(601:end) + 4;
t = datetime(2020, 1, 1, "TimeZone", "UTC") + seconds((0:999)');
[events, info] = timeSeriesPreparation.qc.detectJumps(x, Epoch = t);

Limitations

Quadratic when pruning keeps many candidates, which happens on a long series with few jumps (about 3 min for one day of three axes at 1 Hz with the port). Each step is vectorised over the candidates, whose cumulative sums and F(tau) are cached beside them (X-16).
