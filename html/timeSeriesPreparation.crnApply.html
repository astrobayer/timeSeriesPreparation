timeSeriesPreparation.crnApply

Apply a CRN kernel to an arc, evaluated only at the output epochs.

Syntax

out = timeSeriesPreparation.crnApply(arc, kernel)
out = timeSeriesPreparation.crnApply(arc, kernel, OutputEpochs = t1Hz, Derivative = 1)

Description

Computes out_i = sum_{n=-Nh..Nh} K_n x_{i-n}, the filter of the GRACE L1B ATBD App. B with K the kernel's F, dF or ddF (crnKernel), on the raw-rate grid of the arc, and evaluates the sum ONLY at the requested output epochs (CONCEPT 6.1, item 11). ACC1B at 1 Hz from 10 Hz input needs a tenth of the multiply-adds of convolving at the full rate and sampling afterwards - about 3.6e8 instead of 3.6e9 per day for three axes - and the numbers are the same bit for bit, because every output is the same sum over n in the same order whichever epochs are asked for. For the same reason a long arc evaluated in pieces with half-span overlap equals the arc evaluated whole, bit for bit. The output is sampled, never averaged.

The arc must lie on the kernel's grid: every epoch t0 + j/fs with j an integer (missing epochs are gaps, not an error). An output sample is NaN unless all Nf samples of its window exist, are not NaN and are not removed by a layer of the input: edges, gaps and masked samples never leak into a filtered value. Those samples form the layer "crnWindow" of the result, so a fit weights them zero. The result keeps names, mission, product, release and provenance (Arc.derive); units gain "/s" or "/s^2" for the derivatives.

Accuracy

Each output is one sum of Nf products in a fixed order, so its rounding is that of an Nf-term dot product, a few hundred eps of the largest term; it is independent of which or how many epochs are requested. A constant c comes out as c*kernel.dcGain, a sinusoid at f0 with unit gain; see crnKernel for the kernel's own accuracy.

Example

Filter a 10 Hz constant with the ACC kernel and keep 1 Hz samples.

k = timeSeriesPreparation.crnKernel("ACC");
t = int64(0:36000-1)' * int64(1e8);
aux = struct("obsNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableFieldNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableUnits", struct("lin_accl_x", "m/s2"), "fileName", "ACC1A_2020-01-01_C_04.txt", "product", "ACC1A");
arc = timeSeriesPreparation.Arc(t, ones(36000, 1), aux);
out = timeSeriesPreparation.crnApply(arc, k, OutputEpochs = t(1:10:end));

Limitations

No time-tag correction and no interpolation: an input off the grid refuses instead of being resampled; mapping ACT1A from OBC to GPS time and resampling it to the grid is the caller's (TestCrnVerification shows the chain, X-12). Lifting a model to 10 Hz is the caller's.
