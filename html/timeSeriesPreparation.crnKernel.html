timeSeriesPreparation.crnKernel

The CRN-class digital filter of the GRACE L1B ATBD, as printed.

Syntax

kernel = timeSeriesPreparation.crnKernel(preset)
kernel = timeSeriesPreparation.crnKernel(preset, Tf = 140.7, B = 0.035)

Description

Builds the weights F_n with which JPL's Level-1B processing turns 10 Hz Level-1A samples into ACC1B/ACT1B and KBR1B, and the weights of their first and second time derivatives, from Appendix B of the GRACE L1B ATBD (JPL D-27672, printed pp. 39-40, PDF pp. 40-41):

H_k = sum_{m=-NB..NB} ( sin[pi(k-m)/Nc] / sin[pi(k-m)/Nf] )^Nc F_n = (1/FNorm) sum_{k=-Nh..Nh} H_k cos(2 pi k n/Nf), |n| <= Nh FNorm = sum_{i=-Nh..Nh} cos(2 pi f0 i/fs) sum_k H_k cos(2 pi k i/Nf)

with Nf = fs*Tf (odd), NB = B*Tf rounded, Nh = (Nf-1)/2, and the removable singularity k = m set to its limit Nf/Nc. The power Nc stands on EACH passband term, as printed. Raising the SUM to Nc instead - the v2 port's error - gives DC gain 1.0004533 (ACC), which real data refute: GRACE-C 2020-01-02, mean(ACT1B_y)/mean(ACT1A_x) = 1.000000064 (CONCEPT 8). The printed kernel gives 1 + 1.115e-7. The kernel is normalised to unit response at the J2 frequency f0, not at DC; renormalising to unit DC would change every calibrated scale by the DC gain.

The page prints FNorm's inner cosine with n inside a sum over i, and the derivative sums over i; both are read with the index of their sum. That reading makes the response at f0 exactly 1 and reproduces the numbers above. Filtered data are R_i = sum_n F_n R_{i-n} (crnApply).

Presets (ATBD App. B): "ACC" fs 10 Hz, Nc 7, Tf 140.7 s, B 0.035 Hz; "KBR" fs 10 Hz, Nc 7, Tf 70.7 s, B 0.1 Hz; both f0 = 0.37 mHz. GRACE-FO ACT1B uses a CRN filter whose numbers its handbook (JPL D-56935, PDF p. 20) does not print; the GRACE values are consistent with the measured DC gain above, and the full frequency response is checked in X-12. "LRI" refuses: its numbers are open (V-2).

Accuracy

The taps equal the independent NumPy transcription of the page (tests/oracles/crn_atbd_asprinted.py, ORC-1) to 1e-12 of the largest tap: each tap is a sum of Nf terms rounded differently in the two programs, worst case Nf*eps = 3.1e-13 of it. gainAtF0 is 1 to rounding (2e-16 measured in the oracle). In the passband the derivative kernels reproduce i*2*pi*f and (i*2*pi*f)^2 times the filter response to 1e-12.

Example

The ACC1B kernel: 1407 taps, DC gain 1 + 1.1e-7, unit gain at f0.

k = timeSeriesPreparation.crnKernel("ACC");
fprintf("Nf = %d, DC gain - 1 = %.3g\n", k.Nf, k.dcGain - 1);

Limitations

The ATBD states B as approximate ("~0.035 Hz"); NB is B*Tf rounded to the nearest integer (ACC 4.92 -> 5, KBR 7.07 -> 7). The kernel is built in O(Nf^2) time and memory (16 MB for ACC); build it once and reuse it.
