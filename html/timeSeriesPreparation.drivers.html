timeSeriesPreparation.drivers

Supplied driver series on the epochs of an arc, names and units checked.

Syntax

drv = timeSeriesPreparation.drivers(arc, series)
drv = timeSeriesPreparation.drivers(arc, series, Method = "pchip", MaxGap = [])

Description

The first half of the correlation stage (CONCEPT 6.1 row 9, 7). Drivers are supplied, not computed here: beta' and u from referenceSystem.orbitSunGeometry, the eclipse factor and space weather from NonGravForces, THR1B counts, LRI/KBR SNR. drivers puts each one onto arc.t by align's rule (internal.resampleOnto): a sample on a coinciding epoch is taken bit for bit, elsewhere Method interpolates inside stretches of usable samples, never across a hole (a NaN, or a spacing above MaxGap) and never beyond the ends. An epoch no stretch covers is NaN and marked in gap. Each driver is resampled on its own, so its holes do not remove the others' samples.

Every driver states a name, a valid MATLAB field name distinct from the others, and a unit; an unstated unit refuses (R5). Nothing is averaged per revolution and no physics is evaluated (owner decision 2026-09-24).

Accuracy

That of align: exact at coinciding epochs, elsewhere the method's interpolation error (pchip at 5 s spacing evaluated at 1 s: 3.8e-6 of the amplitude of a 1/rev plus 2/rev signal). A driver with the arc's unit gives align's result bit for bit (TestDrivers).

Example

beta' at 60 s onto a 1 s arc.

aux = struct("obsNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableFieldNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableUnits", struct("lin_accl_x", "m/s2"), "fileName", "ACT1B_2020-01-01_C_04.txt", "product", "ACT1B");
arc = timeSeriesPreparation.Arc(int64(0:599)' * int64(1e9), zeros(600, 1), aux);
tb = int64(0:60:600)';
beta = struct("name", "betaPrime", "t", tb * int64(1e9), "value", 30 + double(tb) / 600, "unit", "deg");
drv = timeSeriesPreparation.drivers(arc, beta);

Limitations

No unit conversion and no frame or time-system handling: the drivers are GPS ns and their units are the caller's statement. Angles are interpolated as numbers, so a wrapping angle (u in [0, 360)) must be supplied unwrapped or as sin/cos.
