timeSeriesPreparation.correlate

Lagged correlation of pre-whitened residuals and drivers, with effective sample size and CIs.

Syntax

res = timeSeriesPreparation.correlate(arc, drv, model, maxLag)
res = timeSeriesPreparation.correlate(arc, drv, model, maxLag, AcfLags = [], BootLags = 0, NBoot = 1000, BlockLength = [], Seed = 0, Stage = "correlate", Level = 0.6827)

Description

The lagged correlation of CONCEPT 7, respecting autocorrelation. - Pre-whitening (Box and Jenkins): axis m and every driver are filtered by the inverse of axis m's ARMA model, e_t = (a(B)/b(B)) (x_t - mu), a and b from noise (owner decision 2026-09-24: the model is the caller's, no fit here). The axis loses model.mu(m), each driver its own mean first. Each finite stretch is filtered from rest and its first p samples are dropped (NaN): exact for AR models; with MA terms the start-up transient decays like theta^k. Rows with weight 0 or filled are missing (CONCEPT 5). - r(l) = corr(x'_t, d'_(t-l)), the Pearson correlation over the complete pairs, for l = -maxLag..maxLag samples; a positive l means the driver leads the residual by l samples. - Effective sample size (Bartlett) per axis and driver: nEff = n / (1 + 2 sum_(k=1..AcfLags) rho_x'(k) rho_d'(k)), n the lag-0 pairs and rho the gap-aware tsmodels.tsAcf of the whitened series. With the residual white, rho_x'(k) is near 0 and nEff near n whatever the driver's own memory; that is why one model per axis suffices. Unwhitened AR(0.95) pairs have nEff about 5 % of n (NumPy, 2026-09-24). - ci: the Fisher-z interval tanh(atanh(r) -+ z/sqrt(nEff - 3)) at Level, at every lag. - At BootLags: a moving-block bootstrap of the pair rows (bootstrap, automatic block length unless BlockLength, seed from Stage and Seed), its sigma and percentile interval. maxLag has no default (R5).

Accuracy

r is exact arithmetic on the pairs. With a white model it equals corrcoef of the shifted vectors; AR(1) pre-whitening returns the innovations to 4e-16 (NumPy). The Fisher interval assumes near-normal pairs; the bootstrap sigma of a lag-0 correlation lay within 0.90-1.10 of (1 - r^2)/sqrt(n) over 100 NumPy realisations (2026-09-24).

Example

A driver acting 5 s late on a white residual: the peak sits at +5.

aux = struct("obsNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableFieldNames", {{'lin_accl_x'}}, "variableUnits", struct("lin_accl_x", "m/s2"), "fileName", "ACT1B_2020-01-01_C_04.txt", "product", "ACT1B");
rs = RandStream("twister", "Seed", 9);
d = randn(rs, 2005, 1);
t = int64(0:1999)' * int64(1e9);
arc = timeSeriesPreparation.Arc(t, randn(rs, 2000, 1) + 0.5 * d(1:2000), aux);
drv = timeSeriesPreparation.drivers(arc, struct("name", "d", "t", t, "value", d(6:2005), "unit", "1"));
white = struct("a", 1, "b", 1, "mu", 0);
res = timeSeriesPreparation.correlate(arc, drv, white, 10, BootLags = [], AcfLags = 5);

Limitations

Linear, pairwise and stationary. The interval at each lag ignores the search over 2 maxLag + 1 lags; pick the lag of interest beforehand or correct the level. Correlation is not attribution: partial correlation and regression are X-09's second part.
