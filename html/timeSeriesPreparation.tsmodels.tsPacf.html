timeSeriesPreparation.tsmodels.tsPacf

Sample partial autocorrelation function (Durbin-Levinson, gap-aware).

Syntax

[pacf, lags, conf] = timeSeriesPreparation.tsmodels.tsPacf(x)
[pacf, lags, conf] = timeSeriesPreparation.tsmodels.tsPacf(x, maxLag)
[pacf, lags, conf] = timeSeriesPreparation.tsmodels.tsPacf(x, maxLag, makePSD)

Description

The PACF identifies the order of an autoregression: for an AR(p) process it cuts off after lag p. It is computed by the Durbin-Levinson recursion on the sample ACF of tsAcf, lags 1..maxLag.

With NaN gaps the ACF is estimated pairwise-complete (see tsAcf), and such an ACF need not be positive-definite; the recursion can then return |PACF| > 1, and a warning says so. makePSD = true first projects the ACF onto the nearest positive-semidefinite autocorrelation, by alternating projection (at most 100 sweeps, to 1e-10) between the PSD cone (eigenvalues clipped at 0) and unit- diagonal Toeplitz matrices (diagonals averaged, rho(0) renormalised to 1). That guarantees |PACF| <= 1. conf is the 95 % white-noise band 1.96/sqrt(n) with n the smallest pair count over lags 1..maxLag. For estimation with gaps prefer tsFitArima (exact missing-data ML); use this PACF for identification. Base MATLAB only.

Accuracy

The recursion is exact up to rounding; a denominator below eps is replaced by +/-eps. The PSD projection stops at a change below 1e-10 or after 100 sweeps. Unchanged from sigtools v1.3.1; TestSigtoolsEquivalence holds it bit for bit.

Example

The PACF of an AR(2) series cuts off after lag 2.

x = filter(1, [1 -0.5 0.3], randn(RandStream("twister", "Seed", 1), 2000, 1));
[pacf, lags, conf] = timeSeriesPreparation.tsmodels.tsPacf(x, 10);

Limitations

The projection is a heuristic nearest point, not a certified Frobenius-nearest one, and costs an eigendecomposition of a (maxLag+1)-square matrix per sweep. Without it, heavy gapping can give |PACF| > 1.
