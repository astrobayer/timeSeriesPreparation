timeSeriesPreparation.spectral.psd2arma

Fit a discrete-time ARMA (IIR) process to a target PSD or ASD.

Syntax

[b, a] = timeSeriesPreparation.spectral.psd2arma(f, S, Fs)
[b, a, results] = timeSeriesPreparation.spectral.psd2arma(f, S, Fs, Order = [8 8], InputType = "asd")

Description

Finds B(z)/A(z) such that x[n] = filter(b, a, w[n]), with w white of variance NoiseVariance, has the target one-sided PSD S(f) of a process sampled at Fs. The scaling is sigtools' S(f) = (2 sigma_w^2/Fs) |H(f)|^2, the same as arma2psd, so the two are inverses. Steps: 1. S is interpolated (log-log, InterpMethod) onto NFFT+1 uniform points from 0 to Fs/2, floored at 1e-12 of its peak. 2. A minimum-phase complex response with that magnitude is built by the real-cepstrum (Hilbert) method. A complex target conditions the rational fit far better than a magnitude-only one. 3. B/A is fitted with sinvfreqz (Sanathanan-Koerner, "invfreqz") or sstmcb (Steiglitz-McBride on the impulse response, "stmcb"). 4. Optionally, poles and zeros outside the unit circle are reflected inside, and the gain is corrected in the log least-squares sense. 5. With Order "auto", na = nb = 1..MaxOrder is swept. It keeps the first order within Tol dB RMS ("tol") or the smallest AIC-like value ("aic"). Base MATLAB only. Since X-02 the function no longer draws; plotPsd2armaFit draws results (owner decision 2026-09-23, clean break; its Display option is gone). The numbers are v1.3.1's.

Accuracy

results.RMSErrorDb and MaxErrorDb measure the achieved fit on the grid. For known ARMA targets, sinvfreqz recovers the generating filter to about 1e-15, and a smooth 4th-order low-pass is fitted to 0.0075 dB RMS (sigtools HANDOFF section 3, NumPy re-derivation). The numbers are unchanged from v1.3.1, and TestSigtoolsEquivalence holds them bit for bit.

Example

A 4th-order low-pass PSD, fitted with an ARMA(8,8).

f = logspace(-2, 2, 500)';
S = 1 ./ (1 + (f/5).^2).^2;
[b, a, res] = timeSeriesPreparation.spectral.psd2arma(f, S, 500, Order = [8 8]);

Limitations

The fit is to the log-log interpolated target, so structure between the given frequencies is invented by InterpMethod. Very steep or notched spectra need high orders. The "auto" sweep uses na = nb.
